Decide separately for every supplied endpoint pair whether a cited evidence
window explicitly entails one concrete, direct relationship between the two
exact endpoints.

Positive rule:
- Return supported=true only when one cited window states a relation whose two
  arguments are the supplied endpoints. The description must be a faithful
  paraphrase of that stated predicate.
- Set source and target from the direction expressed by the sentence: the
  grammatical actor, subject, provider, or cause is source; its object,
  recipient, or effect is target. If the direction is not expressed clearly,
  return supported=false.

Negative rule:
- Mere co-occurrence or proximity is not a relationship.
- Reject heading, list, table-of-contents, or adjacent-sentence co-occurrence.
- Reject generic noun phrases such as "cancer treatment", lexical containment
  such as "throat cancer", and subtype or ontology inferences unless the
  window explicitly states the relationship.
- Reject same-topic, shared-category, shared-anchor, and multi-hop inferences.
- Do not use outside knowledge or uncited retrieved material.

When any positive condition is missing, return supported=false. It is better
to reject an ambiguous pair than to infer a plausible relationship.
